# Short-term, rules-based trading strategies (15-30 mins to several days): evidence, costs and data snooping

## TL;DR
- In the 15-minute to several-day window, only a handful of effects are academically robust. After realistic retail costs, **Market Intraday Momentum in index futures** (ES/MES) is most likely to survive because round-turn costs are around 1 basis point of notional value—5 to 50 times lower than for individual stocks or crypto perps.
- The classic "textbook effects" are either not tradable due to cost (**short-term reversal** - the average net anomaly only ~4 bps/month, Chen & Velikov 2023), have decayed since publication (**pre-FOMC drift** disappeared after 2015; **PEAD** for large caps dead since ~2006) or are statistically fragile (**time series momentum**, **Volatility-Managed Portfolios** unstable out-of-sample).
- Data-snooping nihilism (“nothing remains after correction”) is well supported for pure **unconditional** price-pattern rules (moving averages, breakouts), but the intraday evidence base is thin (at its core, a single two-year study, Marshall et al. 2008). **Conditional** signals (time of day, calendar schedules, gamma hedging) perform systematically better and are the only promising search space.

## Key Findings

**Ranking based on strength of evidence (existence / net tradability / no-snooping probability):**

1. **Market Intraday Momentum (last half hour)** — strong / medium-good (only in futures) / high. Cheapest in ES/MES futures.
2. **Overnight vs intraday return decomposition** — strong/weak-medium/high. Most likely via ETF/Futures, but daily entry/exit costs are a strong pressure.
3. **Intraday periodicity (Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka)** — medium-strong / weak / medium. Individual stocks; costs are prohibitive.
4. **Short-Term Reversal** — very strong (existence) / very weak (net) / high. A prime example of an effect eliminated by costs.
5. **Gamma/hedging demand effects** — medium (peer-reviewed available!) / open / medium. Futures.
6. **FOMC cycle (even weeks)** — medium / open / medium-low (post-publication decay unclear).
7. **Turn-of-the-Month** — medium / weak / medium.
8. **Volatility timing/targeting** — Easy to predict in a neutral direction, but Sharpe growth not robust out-of-sample (Cederburg et al. 2020).
9. **Pre-FOMC drift** — was strong, **disappeared after 2015**.
10. **Short-term PEAD** — for large caps **dead since ~2006**.
11. **Time series momentum in the short term** — statistically fragile (Huang et al. 2020).
12. **Opening Gaps / Gap-Fill** — mostly practitioner folklore, hardly peer-reviewed.

## Details

### PART 1 — Persistence of short-term effects

**1. Market Intraday Momentum.** Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (2018, *Journal of Financial Economics* 129(2):394-414) show on SPY data 1993-2013 that the first half-hour return (from the previous day's close) predicts the last half-hour return. Verbatim: "The first half-hour return, r1, positively predicts the last half-hour return, r13, with a scaled (by 100) slope of 6.94, statistically significant at the 1% level, and an R2 of 1.6%"; combined with the twelfth half hour "the R2 increases further to 2.6%"; on days of high opening volatility "the R2 increases to 3.3% for the combined predictors". The effect is stronger on volatile, high-volume days, recession days and macro news days. **Out-of-sample/persistence status:** widely confirmed. Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021, *JFE* 142(1):377-403) find "market intraday momentum everywhere" in over 60 futures (stocks, bonds, raw materials, currencies) 1974-2020 and thus provide the decisive out-of-sample and mechanism evidence (gamma hedging demand from options market makers and leveraged ETFs). Monash Replication (Limkriangkrai, Chai & Zheng) finds the effect in several APAC ETF markets, but weaker during COVID-19 and absent in Hong Kong/Singapore; verbatim: "the effect is weaker during the COVID-19 crisis period. The absence of the IMOM effect in Hong Kong SAR and Singapore could be explained by the low levels of trading in ETFs and deserves future research." Chinese firm level: Gao et al. (2019, *International Review of Financial Analysis*). Also VIX futures (Huang/Tsai/Weng/Yang 2023, *Journal of Banking & Finance* 148). **Net:** Most tradable in ES futures due to ~1 bp round-turn costs; the signal only trades once a day (entry ~30 mins before close), which means cost-friendliness. **Data Requirement:** ES/SPY intraday bars available under $500/month. **Strongest counter evidence:** COVID weakness; dependence on high volatility; potential consolidation since LETF/options gamma is being discussed publicly.

**2. Overnight vs. Intraday.** Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019, *JFE* 134(1):192–213): For 14 strategies, profits occur either entirely overnight or entirely intraday, often with the opposite sign; They attribute this to different investor clientele (institutional intraday rebalancing vs. retail overnight). Hendershott, Livdan & Rösch (2020, *JFE* 138(3):635–662, “Asset pricing: A tale of night and day”): Beta is paid overnight, not intraday. Bogousslavsky (2021, *Journal of Finance* 76(4)): Size/illiquidity premiums materialize at different times of the day. **Tradability on a daily horizon:** The “overnight effect” requires daily entry and exit; tolerable for SPY/QQQ (~0.3-0.5 bp spread), but not for individual stocks. The cost of daily handling is high relative to the effect — that is the core caveat. **Post-2020 Persistence:** Overnight return patterns in liquid ETFs persist, but the effect is closely intertwined with closing auction price pressures (see 6).

**3. Short-Term Reversal (days/weeks).** Lehmann (1990, *QJE*), Jegadeesh (1990, *JF*). Nagel (2012, *Review of Financial Studies* 25(7):2005–2039, “Evaporating Liquidity”): Reversal returns are compensation for liquidity provision, highly VIX-predictable; Returns close to 1%/day in crises (LTCM 1998, 2007-09), but only ~0.2%/day in calm periods 2004-2007. Da/Liu/Schaumburg (2014, *RFS*). **Counter-Evidence (Central):** Chen & Velikov (2023, *JFQA* 58(3):968-1004) show that anomaly portfolios overweight stocks at ~4x the NYSE median spread and turn over ~40%/leg/month; verbatim: "Net of these effects, the average anomaly's expected return is a moderately 4 bps per month. The strongest anomalies net, at best, 10 bps." Under the original implementation post-2005, the average long-short net return is ≈ -1 bp/month, under cost-minimizing implementation only ~4 bps. Reversal is the prime case where large gross alpha completely disappears due to trading costs. **Cost-reduced variants:** Blitz/Huij/Zhou (residual reversal, Fama-French neutralized) report >8%/year net; Large cap only and liquidity shock only variants (VIX conditional) shift the cost threshold favorably. **Instrument:** Deadly expensive in individual stocks; The question of whether Reversal can be traded more cheaply in index futures/crypto is open - Reversal is conceptually a cross-section effect and cannot be transferred 1:1 into a single futures contract.

**4. Intraday periodicity.** Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (2010, *Journal of Finance* 65(4):1369–1407): Returns repeat at half-hour intervals that are exact multiples of a trading day; Effect lasts 40 trading days. Volume, order imbalance, volatility show similar patterns but do not explain the returns. **Net:** Authors themselves emphasize that timing rebalancing can reduce costs by the effective spread — but as a standalone strategy in individual stocks, the costs are prohibitive. **Replications:** Swedish OMXS30 study (15/30 min.), various follow-up work; the effect is considered real, but difficult to act in isolation.

**5. FOMC Cycle & Turn-of-the-Month.** Cieslak, Morse & Vissing-Jorgensen (2019, *JF* 74(5):2201–2248): Since 1994, the entire US equity premium is earned in even weeks (0,2,4,6) of FOMC cycle time; Odd weeks on average negative. Causally tied to the Fed (informal communication). **Persistence:** The paper itself uses data up to 2016; robust post-2019 out-of-sample confirmation is missing from my sources — beware of post-publication decay. **Pre-FOMC drift:** Lucca & Moench (2015, *JF*; NY Fed Staff Report 512), verbatim: "the S&P 500 index increases on average 49 basis points in the 24 hours before the scheduled FOMC announcement" (t-statistic > 4.5, Huber-White); "a staggering 80% of the annual U.S. equity premium since 1994 was earned in the 24 hours before FOMC announcements" (Sept. 1994–March 2011, 131 FOMC observations). **Findings of decay (clear):** "The disappearing pre-FOMC announcement drift" (sample up to December 2019) finds that the drift **essentially disappeared after 2015**, both in meetings with and without press conferences; possible explanation: reduced uncertainty. This is a prime example of post-publication arbitrage.

**6. Closing auction / market-on-close.** Bogousslavsky & Muravyev (2023, *Journal of Financial Markets* 66): Closing auctions = 7.5% of daily volume in 2018 (from 3.1% in 2010), driven by indexing/ETFs; Closing prices meet the pre-close bid/ask 68% of the time; Price deviations reverse quickly and almost completely overnight. Jegadeesh & Wu (2022): 21% (NYSE) and 43% (Nasdaq) of closing auction returns reverse overnight, cumulative ~61%/85%. **Retail Tradeability:** The Imbalance Signal (Official MOC Imbalance Feeds) is the commodity; the reversal structure (overnight reversal) is a potential signal, but requires access to imbalance data (exchange feed, usually > retail budget or only via specialized brokers) and exact auction execution. Classification: real, peer-reviewed microstructure; Retail implementation complicated by data access and auction mechanics.

**7. Opening Auction / Opening Gaps / Gap Fill.** Bogousslavsky & Muravyev document that opening auctions are **illiquid** and liquidity at the open deteriorates as volume moves to the close. **Peer-reviewed evidence on gap fill/gap reversion as a tradable pattern is virtually non-existent** — this is mostly practitioner folklore. Honest classification: no reliable journal evidence of a robust, cost-surviving gap strategy.

**8. Short-Term PEAD.** Martineau (2022, *Critical Finance Review* 11(3-4):613–646, “Rest in Peace Post-Earnings Announcement Drift”): In modern markets, prices fully reflect earnings surprises on announcement day; PEAD for large caps **non-existent since ~2006**, for microcaps only recently disappeared. Grégoire & Martineau (2021): Pricing occurs largely within the announcement itself (2011-2015), not as a day-long drift. **Counter-evidence/Nuance:** Drift remains partly stable in corporate bonds and microcaps (various follow-up work); the “announcement premium” (increased return on the announcement day itself) and post-announcement volatility patterns continue to exist. For a large-cap focused short-term trader: the classic multi-day PEAD is dead.

**9. Volatility timing instead of direction.** The core statement of the literature is correct: On short horizons, volatility is highly predictable (Corsi 2009 HAR-RV; Andersen/Bollerslev/Diebold Realized Volatility), the direction hardly so. Moreira & Muir (2017, *JF* 72(4)): Volatility-managed portfolios (scaling inverse to the underlying variance) deliver positive alphas in spanning regressions. **Counter-evidence (central):** Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (2020, *JFE* 138(1):95-117) with 103 strategies: Vol-managed portfolios **do not systematically** outperform their unmanaged counterparts in direct comparisons; the implicit trading strategies cannot be implemented in real time, and reasonable out-of-sample versions deliver lower certainty equivalents/sharpes - cause: structural instability of the spanning regressions. Barroso & Detzel: Transaction costs do not survive. **Conclusion:** Vol targeting/position sizing reduces drawdowns and smooths Sharpe (undisputed for momentum crashes: Barroso/Santa-Clara 2015, Daniel/Moskowitz 2016), but a robust net Sharpe increase on hourly/daily horizons is not proven.

**10. Time series momentum in the short term.** Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012, *JFE*) is monthly. Counter-evidence: Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020, *JFE* 135(3):774–794, “Time series momentum: Is it there?”): Asset-by-asset time series regressions show little evidence for TSM (in- and out-of-sample); the large t-value of the pooled regression is not reliable (below bootstrap critical values); The TSM strategy, while profitable, is no better than a strategy based on the historical sample mean. **Short horizon (days/weeks) in futures:** There is no robust, snooping-robust journal evidence for trend following on a daily/weekly horizon that goes beyond the monthly findings; TSM's profitability has been falling since 2008 (Georgopoulou/Wang; Satchell/Grant).

**11. Event-driven situations & gamma.** Ni, Pearson & Poteshman (2005, *JFE*, “Stock price clustering on option expiration dates”): price clustering at expiration strikes (pinning) is real, but difficult to monetize as a retail strategy. **Gamma/GEX:** There is **peer-reviewed** evidence—Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021, *JFE* 142(1)) causally link intraday momentum to gamma-hedging demand; Barbon & Buraschi (“Gamma Fragility,” St. Gallen working paper) and Barbon/Beckmeyer/Buraschi/Moerke (LETF/options imbalances) are working papers. The popular retail “GEX” literature is mostly practitioner folklore, but the underlying phenomenon—that dealer hedging moves prices—is academically supported. Russell reconstitution and index rebalancing produce real price-pressure effects (Greenwood/Sammon), but their dates are known and the effects have largely been arbitraged away.

### PART 2 — Data Snooping on Intraday Frequency

Marshall, Cahan & Cahan (2008, *Journal of Empirical Finance* 15(2):199–210) tested 7,846 technical rules on 5-min SPDR data January 2002–December 2003 using two bootstrap methods; none were profitable after data snooping correction. Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999, *JF* 54(5):1647–1691) found the same for DJIA daily data (7,846 rule universe, White's Reality Check). Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012, *JFE* 106(3)) as well.

**Is the finding widely replicated or is the evidence base thin?** Honest assessment: At **intraday frequency** the basis is **thin** — essentially a Marshall study with only two years of data on one instrument (SPDR). There is no broad replication on longer intraday samples, on 15/30 min resolution, or on multi-day holding periods with clean multiple testing correction. The question is therefore **largely open**, not closed.

**Futures/FX (daily data):** Park & ​​Irwin (2010, *Journal of Futures Markets*): Of 17 US futures markets from 1985 to 2004, only 2 generate significant profits according to White's Reality Check/Hansen's SPA. Hsu & Taylor (2014, CEPR, “Forty Years, Thirty Currencies and 21,000 Trading Rules”) with data-snooping-robust analysis in FX. Park & ​​Irwin (2007, *Journal of Economic Surveys*, “What do we know about the profitability of technical analysis?”) — Meta-overview: early profits, late collapse. **Positive findings (important, often overlooked):** A similarity-based/analogical reasoning rule survives data snooping and transaction cost correction (in- and out-of-sample) in more than half of 19 futures markets. This shows: **conditional**/pattern similarity-based rules perform systematically differently than **unconditional** MA/breakout/filter rules.

**ML/Intraday Predictability:** Gu, Kelly & Xiu (2020, *RFS*) demonstrate economically significant predictability on a **monthly** horizon; Reliable, cost-adjusted evidence for economically significant **intraday** predictability using clean out-of-sample methodology is rare and mostly in working papers with questionable cost assumptions.

**Key answer:** "Nothing remains after snooping correction" is solidly proven for **unconditional price pattern rules** (daily data wide, intraday thin). For **conditional** signals (time of day → intraday momentum; calendar → FOMC; volatility regime → reversal/liquidity; order flow/gamma → hedging demand) the picture is significantly more favorable - economic mechanisms have precisely these effects and sometimes survive corrections. The prospective search space is conditional, not unconditional.

### PART 3 — Cost threshold (quantitative)

**(a) US stocks/ETFs.** SPY/QQQ quoted spreads are consistently **below 1bp (~0.3-0.5bp)**; individual S&P 500 stocks ~4.5 bp per trade (Nasdaq Research). Commissions for zero-commission brokers are nominally 0. **But:** Schwarz, Barber, Huang, Jorion & Odean ("The Actual Retail Price of Equity Trades", *Journal of Finance* 2025, 80(5), 85,417 simultaneous market orders, Dec. 2021 - beginning of June 2022, 6 accounts/5 brokers) find **astonishing spread in the quality of execution**; verbatim: "the average round-trip cost varied from 7 basis points (bps) to 46 bps. As we show, such dispersion is both statistically significant and economically important." TD Ameritrade provided the best prices, two Interactive Brokers platforms provided the worst prices; PFOF **does not** explain the variation. Practical lesson: With zero commission, the broker/wholesaler routing effect is the dominant hidden cost. **Short-Borrow:** Often expensive/not available for retail; eliminates many long-short anomalies. **PDT Rule:** The FINRA Pattern Day Trader Rule ($25,000 minimum equity triggered by 4+ day trades in 5 business days in a margin account) was in effect 2001-2026; it has been **repealed effective June 4, 2026** (SEC approval April 14, 2026, FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10) and replaced by risk-based intraday margin. Until then, it was a tough USD 25,000 entry barrier for strategies with holding periods of less than a day.

**(b) Index Futures.** ES: Multiplier $50/point, Tick 0.25 pt = $12.50; MES: $5/point, tick $1.25 (exactly 1/10 ES). NQ/MNQ analogue. All-in round-turn commission (including exchange, clearing, NFA fees) at discount futures brokers: **ES ~$3.50-$5.00** (Interactive Brokers itemizes ~$4.48 round-turn), **MES ~$1.00-$1.60**, **MNQ ~$0.60-$1.10**. ES spread typically 1 tick ($12.50). **Cost in bps of nominal value:** ES Nominal ≈ Index×50 ≈ USD 300,000 (Index ~6,000). Commission ~4.30 USD ≈ 0.14 bp; Spread crossing round-turn 25 USD ≈ 0.83 bp; **all-in ≈ ~1 bp round-turn**. This is the key finding: Index futures are **5 to 50 times cheaper** per notional unit than individual stocks/crypto because the notional per contract is large. **Margin:** Intraday ~400-500 USD (ES) / 40-50 USD (MES); Overnight Exchange Initial ~13,000 USD (ES). **US Tax:** Section 1256 60/40 rule. **Tax Germany (current status, decisive):** The loss offsetting restriction for forward transactions according to Section 20 Paragraph 6 Sentence 5 EStG (since 2021; only offset against forward transaction profits, cap initially 10,000, then 20,000 EUR/year) was set by the **Annual Tax Act 2024 (dated December 2, 2024, BGBl. December 5, 2024) deleted without replacement - retroactively in all open cases** (Section 52 Paragraph 28). This was preceded by a BFH decision of June 7, 2024 (VIII B 113/23, AdV) with constitutional concerns; BFH decision of March 28, 2025 (VIII R 11/24) on bearing costs. This means that from 2025 onwards, taxpayers in Germany will be able to offset losses from futures transactions against capital income without any restrictions - a decision that is crucial for the net return of a German futures trader. (Technical implementation at custodian banks in some cases only in 2026; check annual tax certificates in 2025.) **CFDs:** were subject to the same (now lifted) restriction; Micro futures via US brokers are practicable for taxpayers in Germany, but i. d. R. without German tax deduction at source (assessment obligation, Annex KAP).

**(c) Crypto Perpetual Futures.** Maker/Taker Base Tier: Binance 2.0/5.0 bps, Bybit 2.0/5.5 bps, OKX 2.0/5.0 bps, Hyperliquid (DEX) 1.5/4.5 bps. BTC/ETH perp top-of-book spreads ~0.5-2 bps (majors, normal conditions; Kaiko/Amberdata). Round-Turn Taker+Spread ≈ **11-13 bps** — significantly more expensive than futures. **Funding costs** for directed positions: typical ~0.01%/8 h ≈ ~11% p.a. in normal-bullish phase (neutral near 0; peaks 30-100%+ p.a. or negative; precise annualized 7DMA values ​​via The Block/CoinGlass); only relevant for overnight/multi-day holds. **24/7 trading:** Advantage for systematic automation (no session gaps, no overnight gap risk), disadvantage for people (operational monitoring) and counterparty risk (unregulated exchanges). **Regulatory EU/DE access:** MiCA covers **no derivatives**; Perps fall under MiFID II and the ESMA CFD product intervention rules (leverage cap for crypto CFDs 2:1). ESMA (Feb 2026): the commercial name "perpetual futures" is irrelevant, they fall under CFD intervention measures. EU/German retail access to Perps on Binance/Bybit/OKX-global/Hyperliquid is therefore **effectively restricted/non-compliant**; Germany ended the MiCA transition period in December 2025. Compliant only via dual-licensed venues (Kraken, OKX Europe X-Perps with term construction).

**Break-even rule of thumb.** Net Sharpe = Gross Sharpe − (N × c)/σ, where N = round turns/year, c = cost per round turn (proportion of nominal), σ = annualized volatility. At σ = 10% and tolerance of 0.5 Sharpe units cost drag (= 0.5% return drag): portable round turns N ≤ 0.005/c.
- ES futures (c ≈ 1 bp): N ≤ **~500 round turns/year**.
- SPY (c ≈ 0.5 bp): N ≤ **~1,000**.
- Individual stocks (c ≈ 5–15 bp including spread): N ≤ **~30–100**.
- Crypto Perp Taker (c ≈ 12 bp): N ≤ **~40**.

Required hit rate: With a symmetrical payoff, the break-even hit rate is ≈ 0.5 + c/(2·average move per trade). The higher the frequency, the smaller the average movement per trade and the closer the required hit rate moves to unattainable values ​​- the fundamental reason why high-frequency price patterns are not viable for retail.

### PART 4 ​​— Statistical requirements and intraday overfitting

**(a) t-value of the Sharpe estimate.** Under the iid assumption, approximately **t ≈ SR_annualized × √(years)** (Lo 2002, *Financial Analysts Journal*, “The Statistics of Sharpe Ratios”). This is independent of trading frequency — **more trades/year does NOT increase statistical power**; only the **calendar time** (years) and the Sharpe count. This is counterintuitive and crucial: an intraday trader with 10,000 trades/year needs as many **years** to reach significance as a swing trader with 100 trades/year with the same annualized Sharpe. **Exceptions under which the formula breaks:** Autocorrelation of returns (overlapping positions, trends), non-normality (skew/Kurtosis — Lo, Opdyke 2007), time-varying volatility, intraday seasonality of volatility. If the autocorrelation is positive, the naive t-value is overestimated; the deflated Sharpe ratio corrects non-normality and multiple testing.

**(b) Required runtime for significance (5%, two-tailed, z=1.96).** T_years = (z/SR)². Multiple testing via Bonferroni increases the z threshold: N=50 → z≈3.29; N=200 → z≈3.66; N=1000 → z≈4.06.

| Ann. Sharpe | Naive (z=1.96) | N=50 (z=3.29) | N=200 (z=3.66) | N=1000 (z=4.06) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 3.8 years | 10.8 | 13.4 | 16.5 |
| 1.5 | 1.7 | 4.8 | 6.0 | 7.3 |
| 2.0 | 1.0 | 2.7 | 3.4 | 4.1 |
| 2.5 | 0.6 | 1.7 | 2.1 | 2.6 |
| 3.0 | 0.4 | 1.2 | 1.5 | 1.8 |

**Bonferroni vs. Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR).** Bonferroni treats all N tests as independent and is massively overconservative for **correlated** variants (e.g. 200 parameter variants of the same basic idea). The DSR (Bailey & López de Prado 2014, *Journal of Portfolio Management* 40(5):94–107) uses the **variance of tried Sharpe estimators** as the effective number of independent tests; if the variants are highly correlated, the effective test number is much smaller than N, and the DSR threshold is **significantly milder** than Bonferroni. DSR additionally corrects skew/kurtosis and sample length (based on the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio). Harvey, Liu & Zhu recommend a t-threshold of **3.0 instead of 2.0** for new factors as a rule of thumb. Minimum Backtest Length (Bailey et al.) and Minimum Track Record Length (Bailey & López de Prado) quantify how long a backtest must be to avoid overfitting selection.

**(c) Intraday-specific overfitting/data error risks (absent from daily data):**
- **Bar Timestamp Semantics** (Bar Start vs. Bar End): Confusion produces systematic look-ahead of a bar length — **the most common and largest source of apparent returns**.
- **Look-ahead through bar close assumptions:** Trading "to the close" of the same bar whose close generates the signal is not executable.
- **Unrealistic fills without quote/order book data:** OHLC backtests assume fills at prices that cannot be achieved without spread/depth.
- **Bid-Ask Bounce in OHLC:** produces pseudo-reversal (Heston et al. point out exactly this as a microstructure artifact).
- **Survivorship & Point-in-Time Universes** at the intraday level.
- **Timezone/Session Errors:** Daylight Saving Time, Globex Sessions, Holidays/Half Days, Roll Dates.
- **Trading Pauses/Auctions:** LULD halts, opening/closing auctions.
- **SIP vs. Direct Feed Consolidation:** Latency and pricing differences.
- **Futures roll & chaining method** (ratio vs. difference adjustment, Panama Canal bias): Incorrect construction creates false trends.

The largest apparent returns typically come from (1) bar timestamp/look-ahead, (2) unrealistic fills/bid-ask bounce, and (3) roll errors.

**(d) Appropriate Validation.** Purged & Embargoed Cross-Validation and Combinatorial Purged CV (López de Prado); walk-forward with embargo; PBO/CSCV (Probability of Backtest Overfitting). **Embargo length:** at least as long as the maximum holding period plus the autocorrelation length of the features — so practical for 15-minute to multi-day holds **several days to ~1-2 weeks** Embargo to prevent leakage across overlapping label horizons.

### PART 5 — Observable approximations of the target population

- **CTAs/Managed Futures:** Bhardwaj, Gorton & Rouwenhorst (2014, *RFS* 27(11):3099–3132, “Fooling Some of the People All of the Time”), verbatim: “from 1994 to 2012, CTA excess returns to investors (i.e., net of fees) averaged 1.8 per cent per annum over US T-bills, which is insignificantly different from zero ... gross excess returns (i.e., before fees) of 6.1%, which implies that funds captured most of their performance through charging fees." Also no alpha compared to simple public futures strategies. Trend following decay also at Elaut/Erdős. Hurst/Ooi/Pedersen ("A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following"), on the other hand, argue for long-term robustness - the debate is open.
- **Systematic vs. discretionary:** Harvey, Rattray, Sinclair & van Hemert (2017, *Journal of Portfolio Management*, “Man vs. Machine”): systematic and discretionary hedge funds achieve similar risk-adjusted performance on average; systematic funds are not inferior. This supports the legitimacy of rules-based approaches, but says nothing about retail short-term traders.
- **Prop Trading & Prop Firm Challenges:** There is little peer-reviewed evidence on the short-term strategies of proprietary firms or the pass rates of "prop firm challenge" providers; available numbers are marketing.
- **Is there a dataset of independent systematic retail traders?** **No.** There is no representative, survivorship-bias-free dataset that exclusively captures systematic, rules-based independent retail traders. The existing retail day-trader literature (Barber/Odean/Lee/Liu Taiwan; Chague et al. Brazil) classifies every intraday trader as a “day trader,” regardless of method, and is the **wrong reference class**. **Conclusion:** The base rate of systematic retail success is structurally unobservable (no central register, self-selection, survivorship). Absence of evidence here is neither evidence of success nor of failure—it requires inference from cost structure, effect size, and statistics rather than an observed success rate.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — choice of instrument and effect (immediately).** Concentrate research on **index futures (ES/MES)** because of the roughly 1 bp round-turn costs and—for taxpayers in Germany—the return of unlimited loss offsetting from 2025. Prioritize **conditional** effects with an economic mechanism: Market Intraday Momentum (the final half-hour, grounded in gamma hedging) is the most promising candidate. Avoid single-stock reversals, which are eliminated by costs, and crypto perps for directional multi-day positions (funding, regulation, and counterparty risk), except as a pure automation/diversification research environment.

**Stage 2 — Statistical Design (before each backtest).** Determine the number of tested variants **in advance** and log them. Require t ≥ 3.0 (Harvey/Liu/Zhu) as a significance threshold and calculate the **Deflated Sharpe Ratio**, not the naive one. Plan for the necessary **calendar time**: An effect with expected Sharpe ~1.0 requires **~13 years** of data for significance with moderate multiple testing (N≈200) — accept that many intraday "edges" are never sufficiently substantiated.

**Stage 3 — Validation (backtest phase).** Use Purged/Embargoed CV or Combinatorial Purged CV with embargo ≥ maximum hold time + feature autocorrelation (several days to ~2 weeks). Explicitly check bar timestamp semantics, fill realism (limit/auction only, no impossible OHLC fills), roll chaining. Calculate PBO/CSCV.

**Stage 4 — Cost Gate (before going live).** Calculate each strategy with realistic costs (ES ~1 bp, SPY ~0.5 bp + broker routing spread 7-46 bps for stocks) and check the rule of thumb N ≤ 0.005/c against the actual turnover rate. If the net Sharpe after costs and after DSR deflation does not remain clearly positive, discard.

**Thresholds that would change the recommendation:** (i) New broad intraday data snooping test over >10 years at 15/30 min resolution that allows conditional rules to survive → stronger trust. (ii) Out-of-sample confirmation of FOMC even-week effect post-2019 → upgrade FOMC calendar timing. (iii) Further compression of intraday momentum in ES after 2022 (shrinking R²) → devalue.

## Caveats
- **No investment advice, no promises of returns.** This is purely evidence-based.
- **Publication bias & post-publication decay** permeate all findings; Pre-FOMC drift and large-cap PEAD are documented examples of post-publication disappearances.
- **Gross returns from journals are caps**; almost all effect sizes quoted are before realistic retail costs.
- **The data snooping evidence base at intraday frequency is thin** (core: a two-year study); The statement "nothing survives" is plausible for unconditional rules, but not conclusively proven for conditional signals.
- **Regulation is in flux:** PDT-rule abolition (June 2026), MiCA/MiFID-II treatment of crypto perps, and German futures loss offsetting (JStG 2024) are recent changes; obtain tax and legal advice before implementation.
- **Conflicting studies were explicitly named** (Moreira/Muir vs. Cederburg et al.; Moskowitz et al. vs. Huang et al.; Bhardwaj et al. vs. Hurst et al.); where out-of-sample confirmation is missing, this is marked.
- **Number context:** All quoted effect sizes with sample/time window in text; The cost calculations in part 3 are based on broker fee schedules (partly marketing, IBKR authoritative) and data vendor spread estimates (Kaiko/Amberdata) and were converted by the author into bps of the nominal.
